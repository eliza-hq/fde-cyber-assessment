# E Corp security assessment — local dry run

**Decision: delay the portal launch.** The direct cross-customer paths tested were denied, but that is a narrow result; relevant published advisories match the installed versions and their fixes and reachability are not established. Sales can read the account list despite the stated expectation. No one owns patching, permissions or security sign-off. The business owner accepts residual business risk after the technical lead supplies evidence and an owner and deadline for each condition.

## Scope, plan and method

The exercise is limited to three focus areas:

1. **Portal data isolation:** whether a portal user can access another customer's orders, invoices or private files.
2. **Internal role separation:** whether Sales User, Accounts User and Auditor access stays within the stated expectations, including Sales access to accounting records and Auditor's read-only role.
3. **Advisory applicability:** whether two or three published advisories related to those boundaries affect this release, considering versions, fix presence, reachability and preconditions.

Assessment began approximately 12:12 CST, 17 November 2026, excluding setup. The target was already running. Time budget: four hours maximum; planned allocation portal isolation 80 minutes, internal roles 60 minutes, advisories 50 minutes, reporting 50 minutes. Sequence prioritized the externally exposed customer boundary, then staff permissions, then source-based advisory applicability. Three small rounds were completed by 12:13; this is a short dry run, not four hours of coverage. We stopped after the maximum three rounds, not because all attack paths were exhausted. Requests were made solely to the supplied local container network; no real data, email, uploads, load testing or external targets were used.

The sample has Northstar Workshop linked to the portal account and Bluebird Outfitters as the other customer. Fixtures identify customer records and two private note files. Administrator was used to identify record IDs and as a positive file-access control. All accounts authenticated successfully. Raw commands and results: `check.py`, `round1.txt`, `round2.py`, `round2.txt`, `round3.py`, `round3.txt` in this output directory. To reproduce round 1 from the repository root, run `sed 's@http://127.0.0.1:8080@http://frontend:8080@' dry-run-output/check.py | docker exec -i cyberfde-backend-1 python`. The recorded run used this substitution: `check.py` itself contains localhost, which would refer to the backend container if passed through unchanged. Rounds 2 and 3 already use `frontend:8080` and can be run with `docker exec -i cyberfde-backend-1 python < dry-run-output/round2.py` and the corresponding `round3.py` command. The externally exposed service is bound to 127.0.0.1:8080. No fixture reset was performed; existing runtime state is an assumption.

## Round 1 — baseline lists and files

Administrator listed both customers, both sales orders, both invoices and both purchase orders, and retrieved both private files (200). Portal REST lists for Customer, Sales Order, Sales Invoice and Purchase Order returned 403; File list was empty; both private file URLs returned 403. Sales could list both customers and orders, but invoice and purchase order lists returned 403. Accounts could list customers, orders and invoices; purchase order list returned 403. Auditor was denied those four business document lists. Sales and Accounts could retrieve both files; Auditor could not. **Lesson:** REST list denial does not establish portal route behavior. Next: use actual portal links and known identifiers, then expand accounting checks.

## Round 2 — portal direct links

As `portal@example.invalid`, GET `/orders/SAL-ORD-2026-00001` and `/invoices/ACC-SINV-2026-00001` returned 200 containing Northstar; corresponding Bluebird identifiers `SAL-ORD-2026-00002` and `ACC-SINV-2026-00002` returned 403. Direct REST GETs of the Bluebird order and invoice also returned 403. The lists `/orders` and `/invoices` returned 200; their rendered HTML did not contain either customer name, so no conclusion about dynamically loaded list contents follows. **Lesson:** the reported direct-link concern was not reproduced for these records and routes; positive own-record controls make the negative result meaningful. Next: test other accounting doctypes and stop after round 3.

## Round 3 — accounting role breadth

Sales received 403 for GL Entry, Payment Entry and Journal Entry lists, but 200 with account names for `/api/resource/Account`. Accounts received 200 for all four (Payment Entry and Journal Entry lists empty). Auditor received 200 for GL Entry, Journal Entry and Account; 403 for Payment Entry. The Auditor GL list contained entries; the Journal Entry list was empty. **Lesson:** “Sales cannot see accounting records” depends on whether the chart of accounts is included. The read-only Auditor expectation was not tested for writes; list results cannot establish read-only status. Stop at three rounds; next validate permissions and write boundaries safely in a fresh disposable environment.

## Conclusions and impact

| Item found or tested | Focus area | Outcome and evidence |

| Auditor and Accounts access | Internal role separation | **Bounded:** Auditor could read GL entries and account names; write permissions were not tested. Accounts could read invoices and GL entries; intended least privilege remains unconfirmed. |

| Two version-matched published advisories | Advisory applicability | **Bounded:** Both are potentially relevant, but fix attribution or presence, vulnerable paths and reachability remain unconfirmed. Details and the interviewer-only source limitation follow below. |

1. **Portal isolation: bounded, medium confidence for tested direct routes only.** Bluebird order, invoice and both customer private notes were denied to the portal account. The own-customer order and invoice controls worked; own private note also returned 403, which may be a functional limitation rather than an isolation failure. The reported cross-customer invoice link is not reproduced, not disproved universally: unknown original link, only one account and two records, no exhaustive APIs, alternate routes, shares, print/export, or dynamically loaded list inspection. If another route leaks a contract or invoice, a trade customer could see another customer's commercial terms or billing information.
2. **Role separation: partially confirmed and bounded, medium confidence for the tested list endpoints.** Sales can read account names, which conflicts if finance means all accounting records; invoice, GL, payment and journal lists were denied. Auditor has read access to GL entries but its ability to modify or submit records remains untested. Accounts read access to invoices and GL entries is observed, not proof of intended least privilege. Sales and Accounts can retrieve customer private notes because they can access those customer records; whether this is intended requires the owner’s decision.
3. **Advisory applicability: bounded, low confidence on exploitability.** Version match alone does not prove exploitation. The interviewer-only `interviewer/private/advisory-triage.md` and `patch-presence.md` were used as leads. **This contaminates candidate realism:** candidates do not receive these files, so this advisory work does not demonstrate what a candidate could independently discover within the time budget. Their prior analyses were not independently verified; exact advisory fix attribution and live reachable paths remain unverified.

## Priorities and launch conditions

1. Assign a named engineering owner for patching and permission review and a business risk owner. Obtain an agreed role and data-access matrix, including whether Sales may read the chart of accounts and private customer files.
2. Identify the advisory fixes and affected code paths, upgrade to a supported patched combination, and test the upgrade and portal workflows. Confirm whether either issue is reachable with the actual portal and staff roles; document compensating controls if a fix cannot be applied.
3. Re-test cross-customer isolation on the upgraded build using multiple customers, accounts, document states, direct links, list APIs, private file attachments and alternate access paths. Test Auditor create/update/submit/delete authorization on disposable records with positive controls and no real notifications. Review Sales accounting permissions and restrict where the agreed matrix requires it.
4. Technical lead presents reproducible results and remaining gaps; business owner explicitly accepts residual risk before launch. Evidence of patched source, successful regression and boundary tests, and signed role decisions would support reconsideration. A verified cross-customer disclosure or reachable advisory would reinforce delay until fixed.

**Next tests:** inspect portal list AJAX calls, sharing and permission overrides; test print/export and file access through other supported paths; test auditor write permissions; verify exact advisory patch and preconditions. Outside the three focus areas, separately review operational logging, recovery and deployment configuration after these launch blockers; these were not investigated.

**Tools and AI:** Codex AI planned and ran local Python `requests` from the application container, inspected fixtures, local inventory and source, and drafted this report. Docker Compose status and curl verified target availability. Outputs were checked against HTTP status, record ownership from fixtures and positive controls; the advisory cache is a secondary lead rather than independent proof. No independent human verification or full coverage is claimed.

**Decision needed:** technical lead: name owners, approve the access matrix and deliver patch and retest evidence. Business owner: confirm the data exposure tolerance and either accept documented residual risk after conditions are met or keep the launch delayed.
